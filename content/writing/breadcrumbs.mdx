Most social media is optimized to maximize time-on-platform. Every mechanic — infinite scroll, likes, notifications — exists to keep you coming back. I've been thinking about what a platform designed around the opposite premise would look like.

Breadcrumbs started from a simple constraint: **five items per day, per group**. Text, photos, short videos — you get five. That's it. After that, they queue for posting at a time the group decides on, and they expire after 24 hours.

## The constraint is the product

Five items sounds restrictive until you think about what it does. It forces you to be intentional. Instead of posting every meal and every coffee, you pick the moments that actually mattered. The feed on the other end isn't noise — it's curated by the constraint itself, not by an algorithm.

This is different from what BeReal or Stories do. BeReal has one moment per day, which is too few and creates a specific kind of performance pressure (you have to capture "the right" moment). Instagram Stories are unlimited, which is the problem I'm trying to solve. Five is a deliberate middle ground — enough to capture the texture of a day, not enough to turn it into content.

## Group-based, not broadcast

Breadcrumbs is organized around groups, not a single public or friends feed. You join groups: your close friends, your family, your teammates, your roommates. Each has its own feed, its own schedule, its own norms.

This reflects how social life actually works. I share differently with my family than with my college friends, and differently with them than with people I play basketball with. Platforms that collapse all of these into one feed make nuanced sharing impossible. You either share publicly with everyone or you share with no one.

The groups are entirely separate. What you post in your family group doesn't appear in your friends feed. The privacy model is simple enough to reason about, which matters for trust.

## Group leaders and the posting window

Each group has a leader who sets the daily posting window — the deadline by which members need to have submitted their five items. Miss the deadline, your count resets for the day. You're not penalized beyond that.

This creates a lightweight commitment device. If your friend group has agreed to share by 9pm, you start thinking throughout the day about what to capture. The platform becomes a reason to be present rather than a substitute for it.

The group leader role is deliberately human. There's no algorithmic feed curation — a person decided when this group shares, and that person is accountable to the group. The leader can adjust the time, let someone off the hook, or dissolve the group entirely. The mechanics are simple enough that the social norms can do most of the work.

## 24-hour expiry

Stories on Instagram expire. BeReal is time-limited. There's something psychologically useful about content that goes away — it reduces the pressure to make things look good, and it makes the sharing feel more like a message than a broadcast.

Breadcrumbs is for your day, not your highlight reel. When content disappears, you stop optimizing for permanence and start sharing how things actually are.

Groups can choose to retain content if they want. A family tracking a kid's growing up might want everything saved. A group of friends sharing their Tuesday evenings probably doesn't. The default is expiry; retention is opt-in and group-level.

## The location trail

The premium tier adds automatic location logging. Throughout the day, your location is tracked (opt-in, obviously). At 7pm, the app automatically posts a "day trail" — a map of where you went — to your premium groups.

This is the feature I'm most excited about. It's automatic, which removes the friction of remembering to post. And it creates a new kind of content: not a curated moment, but an honest record of your day. Where you went, when you left, how long you stayed. It's the context behind the five items.

A day trail plus three photos plus a text note can tell a story that none of those pieces could tell on their own.

## What I don't know yet

The group leader mechanic could get complicated at scale. What happens when the leader is unavailable? Does the posting window need to be fixed? Can it vary by day? I don't have clean answers to these yet.

The five-item limit is based on intuition, not data. It might be too few for some groups, too many for others. I expect this to need calibration in practice, and I'm genuinely uncertain where it settles.

There's also the bootstrapping problem. A social platform with no users is worthless. Getting to critical mass in a small, trust-based network is harder than building the features. This is probably the hardest problem, and I don't have a good answer to it.

## The philosophy behind it

Breadcrumbs is designed around meaningful connection rather than public performance. The mechanics — the limit, the expiry, the group structure — are all trying to shift the incentives away from "make content people engage with" toward "share your actual day with people you care about."

I don't know if it would work at scale. But I think the core idea is right: the best social experiences I've had have been time-limited, context-specific, and with a small group of people. Breadcrumbs is an attempt to make that the default rather than the exception.
